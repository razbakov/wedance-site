User Journey — Meetup Planner

01_Domains/Festival_Experience/Product/meetup-planner/user-journey.md Last changed 7 Apr 2026 — Initial commit: WeDance S3 governance structure Propose a change History Source

What a dancer experiences from buying a festival ticket to having the best festival of their life.

Persona: Festival Dancer (solo traveler, 2–6 festivals/year)

Step 1: Discover the activities page

A dancer buys a festival ticket. The organizer shares a WeDance link in the confirmation email or festival WhatsApp group: "Plan your festival — find rides, rooms, dinners, and dance partners."

The dancer clicks and lands on the festival activities page:

  • Activity cards: Each activity type shows participant count and availability
  • Social proof: Real names and numbers make it feel alive, not empty
  • Urgency: "7 free spots left" creates a reason to act now

Step 2: Sign up with minimal friction

The dancer taps "Join." A short form collects only what's needed:

  1. Name
  2. Email
  3. Dance styles (checkboxes: salsa, bachata, kizomba, ...)
  4. Role (lead / follow / both)
  5. Traveling from (city)

No password. No account creation. Takes ~60 seconds. After signing up, the dancer sees which activities they can join.

Step 3: Join activities

The dancer browses the activity cards and joins the ones that matter to them:

  • Taps "Share a Ride" → sees a carpool from their city, requests a seat
  • Taps "Group Dinner" → joins Friday evening dinner (5 of 6 spots filled)
  • Taps "Find Dance Partners" → sees 3 intermediate bachata followers looking for leads — swipes to connect

Each activity shows who else joined (first name + dance style). The dancer starts recognizing names before the festival even begins.

Step 4: Get matched and connected (the "aha" moment)

24–48 hours before the festival, WeDance sends a message:

"Your festival plan is ready! 🎉

  • Ride: You're driving with Anna and Marco from Munich (WhatsApp group link)
  • Dinner: Friday at 8pm — you, plus 5 others (location revealed tomorrow)
  • Dance partners: 3 matches want to dance bachata with you"

The dancer opens the WhatsApp group. Anna sends "See you at 2pm at the gas station!" Marco shares a Spotify playlist for the road. The festival hasn't started — and the dancer already has plans, people, and excitement.

This is the aha moment: the shift from "I'm going to a festival alone" to "I'm going with a crew."

Step 5: Experience the festival together

At the festival, the matched activities unfold:

  • Ride: The 3-hour drive with Anna and Marco turns into an instant friendship
  • Dinner: 6 strangers meet at a restaurant — by dessert, they're planning tomorrow's beach trip
  • Dance partners: The dancer finds their bachata matches at the social and has their best dances of the weekend

The dancer takes a group photo at dinner. Someone posts it in the festival group. Others wish they had signed up.

Step 6: Post-festival — stay connected, come back

After the festival, the dancer receives:

  • A short survey: "How was your experience?" (NPS)
  • A recap: "You shared a ride with 2 people, had dinner with 5, matched with 3 partners"
  • An invite: "Next festival: Kizomba Weekend Munich — 15 dancers already signed up"

The dinner group creates a permanent WhatsApp group. They start planning the next festival together. The dancer went solo — and came home with a crew.

Aha Moment

The value becomes obvious when the dancer sees:

  1. "I have a ride and a roommate" — logistics solved, money saved, before the festival starts
  2. "I'm having dinner with 5 other dancers tonight" — no more eating alone
  3. "People already know my name when I arrive" — the cold-start loneliness is gone

This connection — which previously required being a festival regular with a big network — happens automatically for anyone who signs up.

What Must Be True

For the first impression to succeed, the system needs:

  • At least 5 signed-up dancers per festival — an empty page kills the value prop instantly. Seed with friends/ambassadors before sharing publicly.
  • One festival with real dates and activities — mock data won't create urgency. The pilot must be a real upcoming festival.
  • Organizer sharing the link — or at minimum, presence in the festival's WhatsApp/Facebook group. Without distribution, no one lands on the page.
  • Quick matching turnaround — if people sign up and hear nothing for days, they forget. Manual matching must happen within 48 hours of sign-up.

Without signed-up dancers, the page is a brochure. The product is the people on it.
